# Batches, the funnel and the posting calendar

One good video is the [workflow](workflow.md). This page is about the **week**: how to make enough videos that you can choose,
make sure they don't all look the same, and post them in an order where every day is a different kind of video.
Every rule here comes from a batch that went wrong; the "Why" column says what happened.

## The funnel: three kinds of video, three jobs

Don't compare all your videos with one number. A video built to be shared and a video built to be saved do different jobs.

| Stage | What it is | Typical shape | Its job | Judge it by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **reach** | Generic, lots of views, you learn nothing, converts nobody, no "follow me" | One relatable idea, under 12 s, built to loop, trending sound or a strong visual interrupt | Put you in front of new people | outlier score vs. your median, shares, 3-s hold |
| **update** | A fast-paced progress update with little information | "This week we…", a milestone, behind the scenes, 10–25 s, a cut every 1–2 s | Make people who saw you once keep following | comments, average watch time |
| **depth** | "Boring" but information-dense | An explainer, a teardown, real numbers, 30–90 s, slower, longer shots | Turn followers into believers (customers, investors, hires) | saves, follows and profile visits per view |

Tag a video by what it **does**, not by its topic: the same product can be a 6-second reach joke or a 60-second depth teardown.
A depth video with 400 views and 12 saves can be a win.

Naming varies: marketing calls reach "top of funnel"; the maintainer calls depth "top funnel" and reach "bottom funnel".
Use the stage names (reach / update / depth) in files so nobody gets confused.

## Rule 1: make 2× what you post

If you post 7 videos a week, brief and build **14**, then pick 7. You choose from real options instead of approving whatever came out.
A good split for 14: **5 reach · 5 update · 4 depth**, so every stage has more options than posting days.

## Rule 2: no two videos alike

**Why:** a whole batch once came out as one video seven times: the same car-dashboard footage, the same text look
(white sans + red script word + small serif line), the same "N things… that's the bar" structure, every caption ending in a question.
Each video was fine on its own; together they were a failed batch.

Give every brief a value for these 10 style dimensions:

| Dimension | Examples |
|---|---|
| writing style | one-liner, listicle, story arc, hot take, confession, comment reply, explainer, numbers only, POV text, tutorial, Q&A |
| delivery | talking head, voiceover, text only, interview, lip-sync to a trending sound, no words |
| edit style | fast montage, single take, split screen, before/after, screen recording + face cam, slideshow, timelapse, POV walk, jump cuts |
| camera | selfie handheld, tripod static, car POV, over the shoulder, product close-up, wide landscape, screen capture, mirror, follow cam |
| text treatment | none, word-by-word captions, headline only, meme top text, notes-app screenshot, handwritten, labels + arrows, bottom subtitles, big kinetic type, the house look |
| setting | workshop, car, office, city outdoors, nature, stage, home, travel, screen |
| hook type | visual interrupt, result first, question, bold claim, … |
| length | under 10 s, 10–20 s, 20–40 s, 40 s+ |
| music energy | none, calm, mid, high, trending sound |
| caption ending | question, statement, "save this", "follow for", cliffhanger, none |

Then check the batch:
- Any two briefs share **at most 3** of the 10.
- No value appears in more than **a quarter** of the batch. Your "house look" in **max 2 of 14**.
- Each stage uses at least 3 different writing styles.
- Nothing repeats last week's writing + edit + camera + text combination.
- At least 8 different reference creators across the 14, one reference per brief.

## Rule 3: a different kind of video every day

**Why:** even a varied batch feels repetitive if the same kind of video goes out day after day.
Fix the week as a calendar, one funnel stage per day, and never the same stage two days in a row (Sunday → Monday included):

| Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat | Sun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| reach | depth | update | reach | depth | reach | update |

When you pick your 7, take one video per day from that day's stage. Two **back-to-back** posts must not share writing style,
edit style, camera, text treatment or setting, and share at most 2 of the 10 dimensions. Swapping a video? Re-check its neighbours.

Hand the batch over as one folder per batch with a `CALENDAR.md` (date, day, stage, suggested video, runner-up), not as
folders dated by post day: your pick sets the day.

## Rule 4: fresh footage, every video

**Why:** a video was rebuilt from the same library clips as the one before, and it looked like a re-upload.
Clips used in a published video are off-limits for the next ones unless you ask for them. No clip in two videos of the same batch.
No new footage? Film a short shot list first; don't fall back to the old library. Real footage only, never generated visuals.

## Rule 5: a cast allow-list

**Why:** someone who should never appear became the face of a video, because the agent picked clips by how they looked.
Keep a list of who may be on screen (with a reference photo each) and who must never be. Check every clip with a person in it
against those photos. Anyone else only as an unrecognisable passer-by. Unsure who someone is → leave the clip out and ask.

## Judging the week

After 72 hours, judge each video **by its own stage's metric** (above) and against your median **for that stage**.
Change one thing per experiment. Keep a short playbook per stage of what is proven to work, and say "goes with", not "causes",
until a test on your own account confirms it.
